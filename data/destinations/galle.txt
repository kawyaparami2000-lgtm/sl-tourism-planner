Galle is a historic coastal city located on the southwestern tip of Sri Lanka, famous for the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Galle Fort. Originally built by the Portuguese in the 16th century and extensively fortified by the Dutch in the 17th century, the fort is Asia's best-preserved fortified European city.

Inside the ramparts, cobblestone streets are lined with Dutch colonial villas, boutique hotels, art galleries, jewelry shops, and atmospheric cafes. Key landmarks include the iconic white Galle Lighthouse, the Dutch Reformed Church, the Old Dutch Hospital shopping precinct, and the Maritime Museum. Walking along the fort walls at sunset provides panoramic views of the Indian Ocean.

Galle enjoys a tropical monsoon climate. The peak dry season runs from December through April, making it ideal for coastal exploration and ocean strolls. The South-West monsoon brings rainy spells from May to September. Outside the fort, nearby beaches like Unawatuna, Jungle Beach, and Thalpe offer swimming, surfing, and beachside dining within a 15-minute tuk-tuk ride.
