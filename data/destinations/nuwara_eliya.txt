Nuwara Eliya, often called "Little England," is a high-altitude hill station situated at 1,868 meters above sea level in central Sri Lanka. Known for its cool climate, colonial-era architecture, manicured golf courses, and manicured gardens, it offers a stark contrast to the tropical warmth of the coast.

Temperatures in Nuwara Eliya frequently drop to 10°C-15°C in the evenings, requiring warm jackets and sweaters. Key attractions include Victoria Park, Gregory Lake for boat rides and jet-skiing, the historic red-brick Post Office built in 1894, and sprawling tea estates such as Pedro Tea Estate and Mackwoods Labookellie.

Outdoor lovers can hike through Horton Plains National Park to World's End, a precipitous cliff dropping nearly 880 meters down to the southern plains, and visit Baker's Falls. April is the peak festival month in Nuwara Eliya, featuring flower shows, horse races, and car rallies. The best time to visit Nuwara Eliya is from January through April when rainfall is lowest and weather is bright and clear.
