Yala National Park, located in Sri Lanka's southeastern dry zone, is the country's most famous and visited wildlife sanctuary. Spanning over 978 square kilometers across semi-arid thorn scrublands, lagoons, and coastal dunes, Yala holds one of the highest leopard population densities in the world (specifically within Block 1).

In addition to Sri Lankan leopards, visitors on 4x4 jeep safaris frequently encounter large herds of Asian elephants, sloth bears, spotted deer, wild boars, mugger crocodiles, and over 215 bird species including painted storks and peacocks. Safaris operate in morning (6:00 AM) and afternoon (2:30 PM) slots.

The main safari hub is Tissamaharama (Tissa) or Palatupana. The best time for leopard sightings is during the dry season from February to July when water levels recede, drawing animals to open waterholes. Yala Block 1 typically closes annually for maintenance and animal rehabilitation during September and October.
