Sigiriya, also known as the Lion Rock Fortress, is a magnificent ancient rock fortress and palace ruin located in the Matale district near Dambulla in the Cultural Triangle. Rising nearly 200 meters sheer above the surrounding jungle, this UNESCO World Heritage Site was constructed in the 5th century AD by King Kasyapa.

The site features intricate water gardens, boulder gardens, and ancient frescoes depicting celestial maidens painted on the rock wall face. Midday climbers pass through the Mirror Wall, covered in historical graffiti, and reach the Lion Gate, where massive carved stone lion paws flank the final staircase to the summit palace ruins.

Nearby Pidurangala Rock offers an alternative, adventurous hike that provides unobstructed, breathtaking views of Sigiriya Rock at sunrise or sunset. Visiting Sigiriya is best done early in the morning around 6:30 AM to avoid heat and long queues. The surrounding area also serves as a base for wildlife safaris to Minneriya National Park to observe annual wild elephant gatherings.
