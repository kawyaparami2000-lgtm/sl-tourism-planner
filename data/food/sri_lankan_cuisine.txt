Sri Lankan cuisine is a rich, aromatic fusion of spices, coconut, rice, and fresh seafood, shaped by centuries of trade and agricultural heritage. Spice profiles feature cinnamon, cardamom, cloves, black pepper, curry leaves, pandan leaves, and roasted red chili powder.

Signature Sri Lankan dishes include:
- Rice and Curry: The staple daily meal consisting of a mound of steamed rice surrounded by multiple vegetarian curries (dhal/lentil, jackfruit/polos, eggplant/wambatu), meat or fish curry, gotukola sambol (herb salad), and crispy papadums.
- Hoppers (Appa) & String Hoppers (Idiyappa): Hoppers are bowl-shaped crispy rice flour and coconut milk pancakes, often served with an egg cooked in the center. String hoppers are steamed rice noodle nests served with coconut milk gravy (kiri hodi) and spicy coconut pol sambol.
- Kottu Roti: The ultimate Sri Lankan street food made by stir-frying shredded godamba roti bread with vegetables, eggs, spices, and chicken or beef on a flat iron griddle using rhythmic metal blades.
- Jaffna Crab Curry: A fiery seafood specialty from the north cooked with roasted curry powder, toasted spices, and moringa leaves.
- Ceylon Tea: Sri Lanka is the world's premier producer of fine Orthodox black, green, and silver tip teas.
